Milestones

Development Timeline

High-level project phases from concept development through testing and final prototype work.

September - October 2025

Research and Background Study

Investigate GPS denial, IMU drift, AI-based navigation correction, and prior research.

Major Findings:

  • Identified critical GPS vulnerabilities in existing navigation systems, such as urban canyons in cities or intentional signal denial.
  • Researched current inertial navigation systems performance characteristics, finding extended drift over short deployments [1].
  • Identified PSO-LSTM and GRU-AKF as the most viable machine learning models for extended IMU drift correction.
  • Established baseline performance metrics for comparison using dead reckoning.

November - December 2025

Requirements and Preliminary Design

Define functional and non-functional system, hardware, and software requirements.

Major Findings:

  • Finalized hardware stack selections, centered around NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano and including high-precision MEMS IMUs and GPS receivers for ground truth.
  • Established primary performance targets: 25+ Hz sampling rate, 6+ hour battery runtime, and <50 ms latency.
  • Implemented MATLAB IMU simulation as a tool for early testing of machine learning models.
  • Specified preliminary software architecture such as machine learning model inputs, outputs and methodology.

January - February 2026

Alpha Prototyping

Develop early hardware and software prototypes and evaluate feasibility.

Major Findings:

  • Designed custom 3D-printed vibration isolation mounts to ensure clean data collection.
  • Developed ROS2 (Robot Operating System) environment for hardware communication in real-time.
  • Trained machine learning models using simulated IMU data and publicly available datasets, as well as investigated low-pass filtering for pre-processing sensor signals.

March - April 2026

Testing and Evaluation

Test system behavior, performance, latency, and deployment readiness.

Major Findings:

  • Logged over 12 hours of synchronized real-world field data across varied dynamic conditions.
  • Confirmed system durability in mobile testing (Kearfott van) and exceeded power targets with an 8+ hour battery life.
  • Identified key areas for improvement in machine learning models and hardware integration.

April - May 2026

Beta Prototyping & Final Delivery

Refine the design and integrate system-level improvements for final demonstration.

Major Findings:

  • Finalized machine learning pipeline and model optimizations with collected real-world testing datasets.
  • Delivered a comprehensive technical package, including hardware, datasets, and software source code for future AI-aided navigation solutions.
  • Packaged the complete operational system with a final unit production cost of <$1,500.

Future Work

Project Next Steps

While the initial scope of the NavFusion project has concluded, here are the three most critical areas for continued development and deployment.

1. Aerial Platform Integration

Transition from ground-based van testing to full-scale UAV flight trials, ensuring the custom vibration isolation and AI drift correction perform optimally under high-frequency drone dynamics.

2. Edge AI Optimization

Further accelerate the PSO-LSTM and GRU-AKF models on the embedded hardware using tools like TensorRT and quantization to drive inference latency well below the current 50 ms target.

3. Multi-Sensor Expansion

Incorporate secondary exteroceptive sensors, such as visual odometry or LiDAR, into the ROS2 pipeline to create a tightly coupled fusion system for indefinitely extended GPS-denied operations.
